\author{Draft for checking (FOCEX notes)}

\begin{abstract}
FOCEX fits the force constants (FCs) to force--displacement data while imposing the translational,
rotational and Huang invariances. With \texttt{enforce\_inv}\,=\,2 and 3 the fit is a LASSO: an $L_1$
penalty on the FCs selects the terms the data support. This note gives the formulation, the numerical
steps and the reason for each choice, and lists the tests the code has passed. In short: the penalty
acts on the FCs themselves, the invariances hold exactly because $\bx$ is confined to the kernel of the
invariance matrix, that kernel is first cut to the directions the data resolve (the same cut the SVD fit
makes), the LASSO is solved by an interior-point method, and, for \texttt{enforce\_inv}\,=\,3, the
selected FCs are refitted by least squares. The $L_1$ weight is chosen by cross-validation.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem}
The unknowns are the $n_x$ irreducible FCs $\bx$ of all ranks fitted (the columns of the fit). The data give
$N$ force equations and the invariances $n_C$ homogeneous ones,
\begin{equation}
\bA\bx\approx\bb\quad(N\times n_x),\qquad \bC\bx=0\quad(n_C\times n_x).
\end{equation}
$\bC$ is rank deficient and has many redundant rows (exact cubic test model: $n_C=1882$, rank 379, $n_x=555$).
Its kernel has an orthonormal basis $\bK$ ($n_x\times n_K$, from the SVD of $\bC$, \texttt{get\_kernel}), and every
$\bx=\bK\by$ satisfies the invariances exactly.

\paragraph{\texttt{enforce\_inv}\,=\,1 (SVD, unchanged).} $\min_{\by}\|\bA\bK\by-\bb\|$ by \texttt{svd\_set}: the SVD of
$\bA\bK$, with singular values below \texttt{svdcut}$\,\sigma_{\max}$ discarded, i.e.\ the minimum-norm
least-squares solution. \texttt{svdcut} $=$ \texttt{force\_error}$^2=10^{-10}$ by default.

\section{What was wrong with the previous LASSO}
The previous \texttt{enforce\_inv}\,=\,2 minimised $\tfrac12\|\bA\bK\by-\bb\|^2+\mu\sum_k n_k|y_k|$: the penalty
was on the kernel coefficients $\by$. The columns of $\bK$ are dense orthonormal mixtures of many FCs,
so FCs that are sparse are dense in $\by$, and a sparse $\by$ gives dense FCs. On an exact model (a pair
potential, below) the FC2/FC3/FC4 had 98/80/376 non-zero terms where the true FCs have 11/20/43, and FC4
was wrong (on-site $-0.43$ for $288$). Its solver (coordinate descent) also never converged.

\paragraph{Alternatives that were tested and rejected.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Elimination basis} (row-reduce $\bC$, the free FCs are a subset of $\bx$; ALAMODE). With the
full constraint set the result depends on which FCs are eliminated (those carry no penalty) and FC4 came
out wrong (error 28 of 288). ALAMODE, with LASSO, keeps only the constraints inside each rank and drops
the rotational rows coupling FC2--FC3 and FC3--FC4 (its own warning, \texttt{alm/constraint.cpp}); that
left FC4 errors up to $1.2\times10^4$.
\item \emph{Free LASSO on $\bx$, then a constrained least-squares fit on the kept FCs.} If the first
step misses one true FC, the constraint rows containing it force its partners to zero in the second:
the reduced kernel had dimension 3 and FC3 = FC4 = 0.
\end{itemize}

\section{Formulation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:classo}
\min_{\bx}\ \frac1{2N}\|\bA\bx-\bb\|^2+\lambda\sum_j w_j|x_j|\qquad\text{subject to}\qquad \bC\bx=0 .
\end{equation}
The penalty is on the FCs. $w_j$ is the rms of column $j$ of $\bA$ (standardisation, as in scikit-learn,
MATLAB and ALAMODE): FCs of different rank carry different powers of length, and $w_j|x_j|$ is what
column $j$ contributes to the forces, so the penalty does not depend on units. Columns the data do not
see at all (Ge: four columns at $2\times10^{-20}$, three exactly zero) get $w_j=10^{-8}\max w$, see
\S\ref{sec:floor}.

\section{Step 1: the part of the kernel the data resolve}\label{sec:cut}
Directions of the kernel along which the forces do not change cannot be fitted. The SVD fit sets them
to zero (minimum norm). The LASSO must do the same: without it, FCs invisible in the data carry
almost no penalty, and through the invariance couplings they absorb arbitrary values (Ge: phonons
of $10^8$\,cm$^{-1}$). So with
\begin{equation}
\bA\bK=\mathbf U\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf V^T,\qquad \bK_r=\bK\mathbf V_r,\quad
\mathbf V_r=\{\mathbf v_k:\sigma_k>\texttt{svdcut}\,\sigma_{\max}\},
\end{equation}
$\bx$ is restricted to $\bx=\bK_r\by$. This is the cut \texttt{svd\_set} makes, and $\bA\bK_r$ has full column rank.
Exact cubic model: 159 of 176 directions kept; the 17 removed are FC2 components on the boundary of the
supercell's Wigner--Seitz cell that cancel in every supercell force. Ge: 134 of 144.

\paragraph{Why not $\bA^T\bA$.} $\bK^T\bA^T\bA\bK$ is small ($n_K\times n_K$) and quick to form, but its eigenvalues
are $\sigma_k^2$: the cut $\sigma_k/\sigma_{\max}=10^{-10}$ becomes $10^{-20}$, below the double-precision
resolution $2\times10^{-16}$. From $\bA^T\bA$ only $\sigma_k/\sigma_{\max}\gtrsim10^{-8}$ can be resolved.
Instead $\bA\bK$ is reduced row by row to a triangular $\mathbf R$ ($n_K\times n_K$) by Givens rotations,
$\bA\bK=\mathbf{Q}_0\mathbf R$; $\mathbf R$ has the same singular values and right singular vectors as $\bA\bK$, and its
SVD is cheap. Cost: about $N n_K^2$ operations (the same as forming $\bA^T\bA$), memory $n_K^2$;
$\bA\bK$ is never stored.

\section{Step 2: variables in which the problem is simple}
Let $\bW=\mathrm{diag}(w_j)$ and $\bz=\bW\bx$ (standardised FCs). Take an orthonormal basis $\bQ$ of the
range of $\bW\bK_r$ (its left singular vectors, $n_x\times r$, $\bQ^T\bQ=\mathbf 1$), so $\bz=\bQ\bt$ and
$\bx=\bW^{-1}\bQ\bt$. Then \eqref{eq:classo} becomes, with no constraint left,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:t}
\min_{\bt}\ \tfrac12\bt^T\bG\bt-\bc^T\bt+\lambda\|\bQ\bt\|_1,\qquad
\bG=\tilde\bA^T\tilde\bA,\ \ \bc=\tilde\bA^T\bb/\sqrt N,\ \ \tilde\bA=\bA\bW^{-1}\bQ/\sqrt N .
\end{equation}
$\bG$ is $r\times r$ and is accumulated row by row, once per cross-validation fold (\S\ref{sec:cv}). The
$L_1$ term is on $\bz$, i.e.\ on the FCs; $\bQ$ being orthonormal keeps the Newton systems below well
scaled. Above $\lambda_{\max}=\|\bQ\bc\|_\infty$ the solution is $\bt=0$ exactly: $\boldsymbol\nu=\bQ\bc$ satisfies the
optimality condition $\bQ^T\boldsymbol\nu=\bc$ with $|\nu_i|\le\lambda$.

\paragraph{Weight floor.}\label{sec:floor} $\bx=\bW^{-1}\bQ\bt$ divides by $w_j$. For a column at $10^{-20}$ the
round-off of $\bQ$ ($10^{-16}$) became FC errors of order $10^4$ (Ge: an FC2 of 153 where the SVD has
$10^{-6}$). Hence $w_j\ge10^{-8}\max w$; genuine Ge columns go down to $3\times10^{-6}\max w$, so only the
round-off zeros are affected.

\section{Step 3: solving the LASSO}
\eqref{eq:t} is the quadratic program
\begin{equation}
\min\ \tfrac12\bt^T\bG\bt-\bc^T\bt+\lambda\mathbf 1^T\mathbf s,\qquad
\mathbf a=\mathbf s-\bQ\bt\ge0,\ \ \mathbf b'=\mathbf s+\bQ\bt\ge0,
\end{equation}
solved by a primal--dual interior-point method (Mehrotra predictor--corrector). With multipliers
$\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol\beta\ge0$ the optimality conditions are
$\bG\bt-\bc+\bQ^T(\boldsymbol\alpha-\boldsymbol\beta)=0$, $\boldsymbol\alpha+\boldsymbol\beta=\lambda$,
$\alpha_ia_i=\beta_ib'_i=0$. Eliminating $\mathbf s,\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol\beta$ from the Newton step leaves one
$r\times r$ system,
\begin{equation}
(\bG+\bQ^T\mathbf D\bQ)\,\delta\bt=\text{r.h.s.},\qquad D_i=\frac{4A_iB_i}{A_i+B_i},\ A_i=\alpha_i/a_i,\ B_i=\beta_i/b'_i,
\end{equation}
which is positive definite ($\mathbf D>0$, $\bQ$ full column rank) even where $\bG$ is badly conditioned;
it is solved by Cholesky. The iteration stops when the residuals of the two equations and the duality
gap $\boldsymbol\alpha^T\mathbf a+\boldsymbol\beta^T\mathbf b'$ are below $10^{-10}$ (relative). It takes 10--16 iterations.

\paragraph{Why this solver.} ADMM (the standard splitting for $\|\bQ\bt\|_1$) had not converged after
200\,000 iterations at 3 of 5 values of $\lambda$ on the exact model. Proximal-gradient methods (ISTA,
FISTA) need the proximal map of $\lambda\|\bQ\bt\|_1$, which has no closed form; on the unconstrained FOCEX
matrix FISTA stopped with optimality violations of 0.3--7\,\% at its default settings. LARS is exact and
fastest for the unconstrained LASSO, but does not handle the constraint. The interior point gives the
objective to $10^{-11}$ of an independent conic solver (cvxpy/Clarabel) at every $\lambda$.

\paragraph{The selected FCs.} $S=\{j: |z_j|>10^{-8}\max|z|\}$. Interior-point zeros are small, not exact; at small
$\lambda$ a few terms that vanish in the exact solution pass the test (against LARS, unconstrained: 57 kept for
46 at $\lambda=10^{-4}\lambda_{\max}$; no true term lost). The test errs on the side of keeping a term,
deliberately: a lost true term makes the refit collapse (\S2), an extra one is fitted to about zero by it.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Step 4 (\texttt{enforce\_inv}\,=\,3): least-squares refit on the selected FCs}{Step 4 (enforce\_inv = 3): least-squares refit on the selected FCs}}
The LASSO shrinks every kept FC towards zero by an amount proportional to $\lambda$. The refit removes this
bias: least squares over the FCs in $S$, all others zero, invariances exact,
\begin{equation}
\min_{\bt}\ \tfrac12\bt^T\bG\bt-\bc^T\bt\quad\text{with}\quad (\bQ\bt)_j=0,\ j\notin S .
\end{equation}
The admissible $\bt$ form the null space $\mathbf N$ of $\bQ_{S^c,:}$, computed by a Householder QR of
$\bQ_{S^c,:}^T$ with column pivoting (an SVD routine did not converge on some of these matrices, whose
singular values cluster at 0 and 1). Then $\bt=\mathbf N\mathbf s$, with $\mathbf s$ the pseudo-inverse solution of
$(\mathbf N^T\bG\mathbf N)\mathbf s=\mathbf N^T\bc$, with eigenvalues below $10^{-13}$ of the largest dropped.
\emph{Limitation:} this step works with the Gram matrix, so its own cut corresponds to
$\sigma/\sigma_{\max}\approx3\times10^{-7}$, not $10^{-10}$; directions between the two are treated as
unresolved here. On the tests below it agrees with a least-squares solution on $\bA$ itself (numpy) to
$4\times10^{-9}$.

\paragraph{When to refit.} With accurate forces the refit gives the unbiased FCs (exact on the exact model).
With noisy forces or too few snapshots it fits the selected terms to the noise; then
\texttt{enforce\_inv}\,=\,2 (no refit) is the safer choice.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Step 5: choosing $\lambda$ by cross-validation}{Step 5: choosing lambda by cross-validation}}\label{sec:cv}
The force rows are split into 5 folds, interleaved (row $i$ in fold $1+\bmod(i-1,5)$), so that every fold
samples every snapshot. For each fold the training Gram matrix is the total minus the fold's, so the
data are read once. On each training set the LASSO is solved on a grid of 40 values of $\lambda$,
log-spaced from $\lambda_{\max}$ to $10^{-4}\lambda_{\max}$ (from the previous solution as starting point), and
the mean squared error on the held-out rows is recorded for the model that will be returned (refitted
or not). The $\lambda$ with the smallest mean error is chosen; within $10^{-10}\langle b^2\rangle$ of the
minimum (round-off, exact data) the largest such $\lambda$ is taken. The final FCs are the fit on all
rows at that $\lambda$. If the minimum is at the end of the grid, a warning is printed.

\section{Tests}
\paragraph{Exact model.} Pair potential $A(r_c^2-r^2)^2$, fcc lattice with two species, ranks 2--4 fitted;
its FCs are known exactly (FC2/FC3/FC4: 11/20/43 non-zero of 99/80/376). Forces from FOCEX's own matrix.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
 & FC2 max error & FC3 max error & FC4 max error & non-zero FC2/3/4 \\
\midrule
penalty on $\by$ (LARS solver) & 28.8 & 0.67 & 288 & 98/80/376 \\
\texttt{enforce\_inv}\,=\,3, exact forces & $9\times10^{-10}$ & $4\times10^{-9}$ & $8\times10^{-7}$ & 11/20/43 \\
\midrule
\multicolumn{5}{l}{1\,\% random noise on the forces:}\\
\texttt{enforce\_inv}\,=\,1 (SVD) & 0.095 & 1.13 & 206 & 98/80/376 \\
\texttt{enforce\_inv}\,=\,2 & 0.012 & 1.35 & 9.2 & 54/61/350 \\
\texttt{enforce\_inv}\,=\,3 & 0.017 & 0.58 & 4.8 & 11/32/136 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
(largest FCs: FC2 80, FC3 48, FC4 288). With noise this data set does not determine FC4 well for any
method; the LASSO reduces the error by a factor 20--40 compared with the SVD.

\paragraph{Ge (FOCEX example, DFT forces).} SVD: fit 0.036\,\%, 171 of 173 FCs non-zero.
\texttt{enforce\_inv}\,=\,3: fit 0.044\,\%, 107 non-zero; FC2 within $2.5\times10^{-4}$ and FC3 within 0.12 (largest
28) of the SVD values.

\paragraph{Solver checks.} Interior point vs.\ cvxpy/Clarabel at five values of $\lambda$: objective equal to
$10^{-11}$; refit vs.\ numpy least squares: $4\times10^{-9}$.

\section{Open points}
\begin{itemize}
\item The refit's Gram-matrix cut (\S7) is coarser than \texttt{svdcut}; a QR-based refit would remove the difference.
\item The selection threshold $10^{-8}$ (\S6) is a numerical choice, tested only on the cases above.
\item BZO and MoTe2 with the corrected code are still to be run, with the phonon dispersions.
\end{itemize}
