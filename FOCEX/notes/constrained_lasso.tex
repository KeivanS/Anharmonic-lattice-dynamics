\title{LASSO for the force constants with exact invariances\\(FOCEX, \code{enforce\_inv} = 2 and 3), and the error bars of the fitted FCs}
\author{FOCEX notes}
\date{11 November 2026 (revision of the note of 24 October 2026; written from the code of that date)}
\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
FOCEX fits the force constants (FCs) to force--displacement data while imposing the translational,
rotational and Huang invariances exactly. With \code{enforce\_inv}\,=\,2 and 3 the fit is a LASSO: an
$L_1$ penalty on the FCs themselves selects the terms the data support; with 3 the selected FCs are then
refitted by ordinary (filtered) least squares. This note derives each step from the starting problem,
says which routine does it (file:routine), and ends with how the standard errors of the FCs are
computed for every value of \code{enforce\_inv}.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem and the exact invariances}
The data give $N$ force equations and the invariances $n_C$ linear relations,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:problem}
\bA\bx\approx\bb,\qquad \bC\bx=\bd .
\end{equation}
$\bC$ is rank deficient with many redundant rows (exact cubic test model: $n_C=1882$, rank 379,
$n_x=555$). \code{svd\_ridge.f90:kernel\_and\_particular} takes the SVD
$\bC=\bU_C\bSig_C\bV_C^T$; the columns of $\bV_C$ whose singular value is below
\code{svdcut}$\,\times$ the largest form $\bK$, and $\bx_0=\sum_{\text{others}}\bv_{C,j}(\bu_{C,j}\cdot\bd)/\sigma_{C,j}$
is the minimum-norm solution of $\bC\bx=\bd$. Every vector of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel}
\bx=\bx_0+\bK\by
\end{equation}
satisfies the invariances, since $\bC\bx=\bC\bx_0+(\bC\bK)\by=\bd+0$; conversely every solution of
$\bC\bx=\bd$ is of this form. So the constraint is removed by fitting the $n_K$ free coefficients
$\by$ instead of $\bx$. Substituting \eqref{eq:kernel} in the force equations,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shift}
\bA\bx-\bb=\bA\bK\by-\bb',\qquad \bb'=\bb-\bA\bx_0 .
\end{equation}

\section{\texorpdfstring{Reference: the least-squares fit (\code{enforce\_inv}\,=\,1) and its filter}{Reference: the least-squares fit (enforce\_inv = 1) and its filter}}\label{sec:svd}
\code{constrained\_fit.f90:fit\_kernel\_basis} minimises $\|\bA\bK\by-\bb'\|^2$ by \code{svd\_ridge.f90:svd\_set}.
With the thin SVD $\bA\bK=\bU\bSig\bV^T=\sum_k\sigma_k\bu_k\bv_k^T$, write $\by=\sum_k y_k\bv_k$ and
$\beta_k=\bu_k\cdot\bb'$. Because the $\bu_k$ are orthonormal,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decouple}
\|\bA\bK\by-\bb'\|^2=\sum_k(\sigma_ky_k-\beta_k)^2+\|\bb'\|^2-\sum_k\beta_k^2 ,
\end{equation}
so each $y_k$ is fitted independently: $y_k=\beta_k/\sigma_k$. This is unstable when $\sigma_k$ is small
(noise in $\beta_k$ is divided by $\sigma_k$). \code{svbksb} uses instead
\begin{equation}\label{eq:filter}
y_k=\phi_k\,\frac{\beta_k}{\sigma_k},\qquad \phi_k=\frac{\sigma_k^3}{\sqrt{\sigma_k^6+\sigma_c^6}},\qquad
\sigma_c=\text{cut}\times\sigma_{\max},
\end{equation}
so $\phi_k\to1$ for $\sigma_k\gg\sigma_c$ and $y_k\to\sigma_k^2\beta_k/\sigma_c^3\to0$ for $\sigma_k\ll\sigma_c$.
The cut is \code{svdcut}\,=\,(\code{default.params} line 2)$^2$, $10^{-10}$ by default, possibly moved
up into a gap of the singular values (\code{svd\_ridge.f90:gap\_cut}), and raised once more if the
measured noise $\|\bA\bx-\bb\|^2/\|\bb\|^2$ exceeds $100\,$\code{svdcut} and the result passes the
noise test of \S\ref{sec:noise} (\code{svd\_set}).

\paragraph{The filter is a quadratic penalty.} Minimising $\tfrac12(\sigma_ky_k-\beta_k)^2+\tfrac12g_ky_k^2$ gives
$y_k=\sigma_k\beta_k/(\sigma_k^2+g_k)$. This equals \eqref{eq:filter},
$y_k=\sigma_k^2\beta_k/\sqrt{\sigma_k^6+\sigma_c^6}$, when $\sigma_k^2+g_k=\sqrt{\sigma_k^6+\sigma_c^6}/\sigma_k$, i.e.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gk}
g_k=\sigma_k^2\Big(\sqrt{1+(\sigma_c/\sigma_k)^6}-1\Big)\qquad
\big(\approx\sigma_c^6/2\sigma_k^4\ \text{for}\ \sigma_k\gg\sigma_c,\ \ \approx\sigma_c^3/\sigma_k\ \text{for}\ \sigma_k\ll\sigma_c\big).
\end{equation}
The LASSO uses this form (\S\ref{sec:step1}): it reproduces the least-squares fit exactly when $\lambda=0$.

\section{The constrained LASSO}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:classo}
\min_{\bx}\ \frac1{2N}\|\bA\bx-\bb\|^2+\lambda\sum_jw_j|x_j|\qquad\text{subject to}\qquad\bC\bx=\bd .
\end{equation}
The penalty is on the FCs $x_j$, not on the kernel coefficients $y_k$: each column of $\bK$ mixes many FCs, so
a sparse $\by$ is a dense $\bx$ and vice versa (\S\ref{sec:why}). The weight $w_j=\big(\sum_i A_{ij}^2/N\big)^{1/2}$
(\code{lasso\_constrained.f90:classo\_setup}) is the rms force that FC $j$ produces per unit value, so
$w_j|x_j|$ is in units of force for every rank, and the penalty does not depend on the units of each
rank. Columns the data do not see are floored, $w_j\ge10^{-8}\max w$ (\S\ref{sec:step2}).
The code solves \eqref{eq:classo} in five steps (\code{classo\_cv}, \code{classo\_cv\_res}).

\section{Step 1: rotate the kernel onto the singular directions of the data}\label{sec:step1}
\code{lasso\_constrained.f90:resolved\_kernel} computes the SVD $\bA\bK=\bU\bSig\bV^T$ of \S\ref{sec:svd} and returns
\begin{equation}
\bK_r=\bK\bV_r,\qquad \bV_r=\{\bv_k:\ \sigma_k>10^{-15}\sigma_{\max}\},
\end{equation}
i.e.\ only exactly-null directions (forces do not change at all) are dropped; $r$ is their number.
$\bK_r$ is again an orthonormal basis of (the resolved part of) the kernel, because
$\bK_r^T\bK_r=\bV_r^T\bK^T\bK\bV_r=\mathbf 1$, so \eqref{eq:kernel} becomes $\bx=\bx_0+\bK_r\by$, with $\by$ now
the coordinates along the $\bv_k$. In these coordinates \eqref{eq:decouple} holds term by term, and the
filter of \S\ref{sec:svd} is the diagonal penalty $\frac1{2N}\sum_kg_ky_k^2$ with $g_k$ from \eqref{eq:gk}
(\code{classo\_cv\_res:filter\_penalty}). Without it, FCs invisible in the data cost almost nothing
and absorb arbitrary values through the invariance couplings (Ge: phonons of $10^8$\,cm$^{-1}$).

\paragraph{How $\bV$ is obtained without squaring $\sigma$ ($\bH$ and $\bR$).} Forming $\bK^T\bA^T\bA\bK$ would be cheap, but its
eigenvalues are $\sigma_k^2$: a ratio $\sigma_k/\sigma_{\max}=10^{-10}$ becomes $10^{-20}$, below the double-precision
resolution $2\times10^{-16}$. Instead the rows of $\bA\bK$ are fed one at a time and absorbed into an
upper-triangular $r\times r$ matrix $\bR$ by Givens rotations (2$\times$2 rotations that zero one
element of the incoming row against the diagonal of $\bR$). The product of all rotations is an
orthogonal $\bH$ ($N\times N$, never stored) with
$\bA\bK=\bH\begin{pmatrix}\bR\\0\end{pmatrix}$. If $\bR=\bU_R\bSig\bV^T$ (\code{svdcmp}, cheap: $r\times r$), then
$\bA\bK=\big(\bH\begin{smallmatrix}\bU_R\\0\end{smallmatrix}\big)\bSig\bV^T$, and the first factor has orthonormal columns;
so $\bR$ has the same singular values and right singular vectors $\bV$ as $\bA\bK$, without any squaring.
Cost about $Nn_K^2$, memory $n_K^2$; $\bA\bK$ is never stored.

\section{Step 2: variables in which the problem has no constraint and a simple penalty}\label{sec:step2}
After step 1, \eqref{eq:classo} reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iny}
\min_{\by}\ \frac1{2N}\|\bA\bK_r\by-\bb'\|^2+\frac1{2N}\sum_kg_ky_k^2+\lambda\|\bW(\bx_0+\bK_r\by)\|_1 .
\end{equation}
The constraint is gone, but the $L_1$ term is a norm of a linear map of $\by$. Three changes of variable make it simpler.

\emph{(a) Standardised FCs.} $\bz=\bW\bx$, so $\sum_jw_j|x_j|=\|\bz\|_1$ and
$\bz=\bz_0+\bW\bK_r\by$, $\bz_0=\bW\bx_0$.

\emph{(b) An orthonormal basis of the reachable $\bz$.} The vectors $\bW\bK_r\by$ span the range of the
$n_x\times r$ matrix $\bW\bK_r$. Take its thin SVD (\code{classo\_setup}),
$\bW\bK_r=\bQ\,\bSig_W\bV_W^T$, with $\bQ$ ($n_x\times r$, $\bQ^T\bQ=\mathbf 1$) its left singular vectors, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:t}
\bt=\bB\by,\qquad \bB=\bQ^T\bW\bK_r=\bSig_W\bV_W^T\ \ (r\times r,\ \text{invertible}),\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\bz=\bz_0+\bQ\bt,\quad \bx=\bx_0+\bW^{-1}\bQ\bt .
\end{equation}
$\bB$ is invertible because $\bW$ is diagonal positive and $\bK_r$ has orthonormal columns, so $\bW\bK_r$ has
full column rank (the code checks $\min\sigma_W>10^{-12}\max\sigma_W$, \code{info}\,=\,1 otherwise). The penalty
becomes $\lambda\|\bz_0+\bQ\bt\|_1$, and the orthonormal $\bQ$ keeps the Newton systems of step 3 well scaled
(any basis of the range would do mathematically).

\emph{(c) The data term in $\bt$.} With \eqref{eq:shift} and \eqref{eq:t}, $\bA\bx-\bb=\bA\bW^{-1}\bQ\bt-\bb'$. Define
$\tilde\bA=\bA\bW^{-1}\bQ/\sqrt N$ ($N\times r$) and expand the square:
\begin{equation}
\frac1{2N}\|\bA\bx-\bb\|^2=\tfrac12\|\tilde\bA\bt-\bb'/\sqrt N\|^2
=\tfrac12\bt^T\bG\bt-\bc^T\bt+\tfrac1{2N}\|\bb'\|^2,\qquad \bG=\tilde\bA^T\tilde\bA,\ \ \bc=\tilde\bA^T\bb'/\sqrt N .
\end{equation}
$\bG$ and $\bc$ are accumulated row by row, $\bG=\sum_i\tilde\ba_i\tilde\ba_i^T$ with
$\tilde\ba_i=\bQ^T(\bA_{i,:}/\bw)^T/\sqrt N$, separately for each cross-validation fold (step 5).
The filter penalty in $\bt$: $\by=\bB^{-1}\bt$, so $\frac1{2N}\sum_kg_ky_k^2=\tfrac12\bt^T\bP\bt$ with
$\bP=\bB^{-T}\mathrm{diag}(g_k)\bB^{-1}/N$. Collecting, \eqref{eq:classo} is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tproblem}
\boxed{\ \min_{\bt}\ \tfrac12\bt^T(\bG+\bP)\bt-\bc^T\bt+\lambda\|\bz_0+\bQ\bt\|_1\ }
\end{equation}
with no constraint: every $\bt$ gives FCs that satisfy the invariances exactly.

\paragraph{The largest useful $\lambda$.} Take $\bz_0=0$. At $\bt=0$ the $L_1$ term is not differentiable; $\bt=0$ is the minimum
if $0$ is in the subgradient, $0\in-\bc+\lambda\bQ^T\boldsymbol\nu$ with $|\nu_i|\le1$. The choice
$\boldsymbol\nu=\bQ\bc/\lambda$ satisfies $\bQ^T\boldsymbol\nu=\bQ^T\bQ\bc/\lambda=\bc/\lambda$, and $|\nu_i|\le1$ when
$\lambda\ge\lambda_{\max}\equiv\|\bQ\bc\|_\infty$. So for $\lambda\ge\lambda_{\max}$ all FCs are zero (this is a sufficient condition;
the grid of step 5 starts there). With the L2 option (\S\ref{sec:l2}) $\bc$ is replaced by $\bc-\bc_{pz}$.

\paragraph{Weight floor.} $\bx=\bx_0+\bW^{-1}\bQ\bt$ divides by $w_j$. For a column at $10^{-20}$ the round-off of
$\bQ$ ($10^{-16}$) became FC errors of order $10^4$ (Ge: an FC2 of 153 where the SVD has $10^{-6}$).
Hence $w_j\ge10^{-8}\max w$; genuine Ge columns go down to $3\times10^{-6}\max w$, so only round-off zeros are affected.

\section{Step 3: solving \eqref{eq:tproblem} by an interior-point method}\label{sec:ipm}
\code{lasso\_constrained.f90:ipm\_classo}. Write $\bz=\bz_0+\bQ\bt$ and introduce $\bs\ge|\bz|$ componentwise, i.e.\
$\mathbf a=\bs-\bz\ge0$, $\mathbf b=\bs+\bz\ge0$. Since the objective increases with $s_i$, at the optimum $s_i=|z_i|$, and
\eqref{eq:tproblem} is the smooth quadratic program
\begin{equation}
\min_{\bt,\bs}\ \tfrac12\bt^T\bG\bt-\bc^T\bt+\lambda\mathbf 1^T\bs,\qquad\mathbf a=\bs-\bz\ge0,\ \ \mathbf b=\bs+\bz\ge0
\end{equation}
($\bG$ here stands for $\bG+\bP$). With multipliers $\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol\beta\ge0$, the Lagrangian
$\mathcal L=\tfrac12\bt^T\bG\bt-\bc^T\bt+\lambda\mathbf 1^T\bs-\boldsymbol\alpha^T\mathbf a-\boldsymbol\beta^T\mathbf b$ gives the optimality (KKT) conditions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kkt}
\partial_{\bt}:\ \bG\bt-\bc+\bQ^T(\boldsymbol\alpha-\boldsymbol\beta)=0,\qquad
\partial_{\bs}:\ \boldsymbol\alpha+\boldsymbol\beta=\lambda,\qquad
\alpha_ia_i=\beta_ib_i=0 .
\end{equation}
The interior-point method replaces the last pair by $\alpha_ia_i=\beta_ib_i=\mu>0$ and drives $\mu\to0$, taking Newton
steps on \eqref{eq:kkt}. Linearising, with $\delta\mathbf a=\delta\bs-\bQ\delta\bt$, $\delta\mathbf b=\delta\bs+\bQ\delta\bt$
and residuals $\mathbf r_t,\ r_s=\lambda-\alpha-\beta,\ r_a,\ r_b$:
\begin{align*}
&\alpha_i\delta a_i+a_i\delta\alpha_i=r_{a,i}\ \Rightarrow\ \delta\alpha_i=r_{a,i}/a_i-A_i\delta a_i,\qquad A_i=\alpha_i/a_i,\\
&\beta_i\delta b_i+b_i\delta\beta_i=r_{b,i}\ \Rightarrow\ \delta\beta_i=r_{b,i}/b_i-B_i\delta b_i,\qquad B_i=\beta_i/b_i .
\end{align*}
Inserting these in $\delta\alpha_i+\delta\beta_i=r_{s,i}$ gives $\delta s_i=(e_i+(A_i-B_i)q_i)/(A_i+B_i)$ with $q_i=(\bQ\delta\bt)_i$,
$e_i=r_{a,i}/a_i+r_{b,i}/b_i-r_{s,i}$; then
$\delta\alpha_i-\delta\beta_i=f_i+\frac{(A_i+B_i)^2-(A_i-B_i)^2}{A_i+B_i}q_i=f_i+D_iq_i$ with
$f_i=r_{a,i}/a_i-r_{b,i}/b_i-(A_i-B_i)e_i/(A_i+B_i)$ and
\begin{equation}
D_i=\frac{4A_iB_i}{A_i+B_i}>0 .
\end{equation}
The first equation of \eqref{eq:kkt} then becomes one $r\times r$ system,
\begin{equation}
(\bG+\bQ^T\bD\bQ)\,\delta\bt=-\mathbf r_t-\bQ^T\mathbf f ,
\end{equation}
positive definite even where $\bG$ is badly conditioned ($\bD>0$, $\bQ$ full column rank), solved by Cholesky
(\code{chol\_fac}, \code{chol\_sol}); $\delta\bs,\delta\boldsymbol\alpha,\delta\boldsymbol\beta$ follow from the lines above.
Each iteration does it twice (Mehrotra predictor--corrector): a predictor with $r_a=-\alpha a$, $r_b=-\beta b$, then a
corrector with $r_a=\sigma\mu-\alpha a-\delta\alpha^{\rm pred}\delta a^{\rm pred}$ (same for $b$), $\sigma=(\mu_{\rm pred}/\mu)^3$;
the step is $0.99$ of the largest one that keeps $\mathbf a,\mathbf b,\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol\beta>0$. It stops when the residuals of
\eqref{eq:kkt} and the duality gap $\boldsymbol\alpha^T\mathbf a+\boldsymbol\beta^T\mathbf b$ are below $10^{-10}$ (relative), in at most 300
iterations (10--16 in practice). For $\lambda\ge\|\bQ\bc\|_\infty$ and $\bz_0=0$ it returns $\bt=0$ directly.

\paragraph{The selected FCs.} $S=\{j:\ |z_j|>10^{-8}\max|z|\}$ (\code{classo\_path}). Interior-point zeros are small, not exact; at small
$\lambda$ a few terms that vanish in the exact solution pass the test (against LARS, unconstrained: 57 kept for 46 at
$\lambda=10^{-4}\lambda_{\max}$; no true term lost). The test errs on the side of keeping a term: a lost true term makes the
refit collapse (\S\ref{sec:why}), an extra one is fitted to about zero by it.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Step 4 (\code{enforce\_inv}\,=\,3): the refit on the selected FCs}{Step 4 (enforce\_inv = 3): the refit on the selected FCs}}\label{sec:refit}
\emph{What it means.} Step 3 does two things at once: it \emph{selects} the FCs in $S$ (the others are zero) and it
\emph{shrinks} the values of those in $S$. The refit keeps the selection and discards the values: it solves the
least-squares problem again, without the $L_1$ term, with the FCs outside $S$ fixed at zero and the invariances still exact.

\emph{Why the values are biased.} In one orthonormal direction the problem is $\min_x\tfrac12(x-\beta)^2+\lambda|x|$, whose
solution is $x=\mathrm{sign}(\beta)\max(|\beta|-\lambda,0)$: every kept coefficient is too small in magnitude by $\lambda$.
The least-squares value is $\beta$. The refit removes this bias (relaxed lasso, Meinshausen 2007).

\emph{The equations} (\code{lasso\_constrained.f90:refit\_support}). Drop the $L_1$ term from \eqref{eq:tproblem} and impose
$z_j=(\bz_0+\bQ\bt)_j=0$ for $j\notin S$. With $\bB_S=\bQ_{S^c,:}$ (the $m$ rows of $\bQ$ off the support, $m=|S^c|$) the
constraint is $\bB_S\bt=-\bz_{0,S^c}$. Its solutions are
\begin{equation}
\bt=\bt_p+\bN\bs,\qquad \bB_S\bN=0,\ \ \bN^T\bN=\mathbf 1,
\end{equation}
with $\bt_p$ one solution (0 if $\bz_0=0$) and the columns of $\bN$ a basis of the null space of $\bB_S$ ($p$ free parameters).
Substituting in $\tfrac12\bt^T\bG\bt-\bc^T\bt$ ($\bG$ again including $\bP$) and dropping constants,
\begin{equation}
\tfrac12\bs^T(\bN^T\bG\bN)\bs-\big(\bN^T(\bc-\bG\bt_p)\big)^T\bs
\ \Rightarrow\ (\bN^T\bG\bN)\,\bs=\bN^T(\bc-\bG\bt_p) ,
\end{equation}
solved by the pseudo-inverse with eigenvalues below $10^{-13}$ of the largest dropped. Because $\bP$ is included, this is the
filtered least squares of \S\ref{sec:svd} restricted to $S$; with $S$ = all FCs it is \code{svd\_set}'s fit.

\emph{The null space.} $\bN$ comes from a Householder QR of $\bB_S^T$ ($r\times m$) with column pivoting
(\code{null\_space\_qr}): $\bB_S^T\boldsymbol\Pi=\bH_{\rm QR}\begin{pmatrix}\bR_{11}&\bR_{12}\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$, the rank $\rho$ being where the largest remaining
column norm drops below $10^{-9}$ of the largest. Then $\bB_S=\boldsymbol\Pi\begin{pmatrix}\bR_{11}^T&0\\\bR_{12}^T&0\end{pmatrix}\bH_{\rm QR}^T$, so the columns
$\rho+1,\dots,r$ of the orthogonal $\bH_{\rm QR}$ satisfy $\bB_S\mathbf h_j=0$: they are $\bN$, $p=r-\rho$. (An SVD routine did not converge
on some $\bB_S$, whose singular values cluster at 0 and 1.)

\paragraph{When to refit.} With accurate forces the refit gives unbiased FCs (exact on the exact model). With noisy forces or too
few snapshots it fits the selected terms to the noise; then \code{enforce\_inv}\,=\,2 (no refit) is the safer choice.
\emph{Limitation:} the refit works with the Gram matrix $\bN^T\bG\bN$, so its cut $10^{-13}$ on eigenvalues corresponds to
$\sigma/\sigma_{\max}\approx3\times10^{-7}$; directions between this and \code{svdcut} are treated as unresolved here.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Step 5: choosing $\lambda$}{Step 5: choosing lambda}}\label{sec:cv}
\paragraph{Grid.} 40 values (\code{common/params.f90}: \code{lasso\_nlambda}), log-spaced from $\lambda_{\max}$ to $10^{-4}\lambda_{\max}$
(\code{lasso\_epsratio}); the path is solved from large to small $\lambda$, and the refit is redone only when $S$ changes.

\paragraph{Cross-validation (default, \code{default.params} line 6 absent or $\ne1$).} The force rows are split into 5 folds,
interleaved (row $i$ in fold $1+\bmod(i-1,5)$), so that every fold samples every snapshot. Since $\bG=\sum_f\bG_f$ over the
folds, the training data of fold $f$ are $\bG-\bG_f$, $\bc-\bc_f$ (no new pass over the data); multiplying by $N/(N-n_f)$
restores the $1/(2N_{\rm train})$ normalisation of \eqref{eq:classo}. The held-out error is the mean squared residual per
row of fold $f$, $(\bt^T\bG_f\bt-2\bc_f^T\bt+\|\bb'_f\|^2/N)\,N/n_f$, for the model that is returned (the refit for 3, the
LASSO for 2). The $\lambda$ of smallest mean error is chosen; within $10^{-13}\langle b^2\rangle$ of the minimum (round-off ties,
exact data) the largest such $\lambda$. If the minimum is at the end of the grid, the grid is extended by two decades at the
same density, down to $10^{-8}\lambda_{\max}$ (\code{classo\_cv}). The final FCs are the fit on all rows at that $\lambda$.

\paragraph{Noise test (\code{default.params} line 6 = 1).}\label{sec:noise} First the full fit on all directions ($S$ = all, the
filtered least squares) gives the residual $\mathrm{RSS}_{\rm full}$, its effective number of parameters
$p_{\rm full}=\mathrm{tr}$ of the hat matrix, and the noise $s^2=\mathrm{RSS}_{\rm full}/(N-p_{\rm full})$. If the forces are
Gaussian with variance $s^2$, dropping $\Delta p$ parameters that are truly zero raises the residual by $s^2\chi^2_{\Delta p}$,
of mean $\Delta p$ and variance $2\Delta p$. The chosen $\lambda$ is the largest one whose refit on all rows passes
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RSS}(\lambda)-\mathrm{RSS}_{\rm full}\le s^2\big(\Delta p+2\sqrt{2\Delta p}\big)
\end{equation}
(mean $+$ 2 standard deviations). The same test decides whether \code{svd\_set} and \code{classo\_cv\_res} may raise the
filter cut when the measured noise is larger than line 2 assumed.

\paragraph{Optional L2 term (\code{default.params} line 7 = 1): a Gaussian prior.}\label{sec:l2} If the forces have Gaussian noise
of variance $s^2$ and each FC of rank $\rho$ a Gaussian prior of width $\tau_\rho$, the negative log posterior is
$\|\bA\bx-\bb\|^2/(2s^2)+\sum_jx_j^2/(2\tau_{\rho(j)}^2)$; multiplied by $s^2/N$ it is the data term of \eqref{eq:classo}
plus $\frac{s^2}N\sum_jx_j^2/(2\tau_{\rho(j)}^2)$. The code adds this term (maximum a posteriori, MAP), with $s^2$ from the full
fit above and $\tau_\rho$ = rms of the rank-$\rho$ FCs of that fit. In $\bt$ (with $x_j=z_j/w_j$, $\bz=\bz_0+\bQ\bt$) it is
$\tfrac12\bt^T\bQ^T\bD_2\bQ\bt+(\bQ^T\bD_2\bz_0)^T\bt$ + const, $D_{2,j}=s^2/(Nw_j^2\tau_j^2)$, so $\bP\to\bP+\bQ^T\bD_2\bQ$
and $\bc\to\bc-\bc_{pz}$, $\bc_{pz}=\bQ^T\bD_2\bz_0$. It enters the LASSO, the refits and the training fits; the CV errors and
the noise test use the force residuals alone. (The $L_1$ term itself is the MAP of a Laplace prior.)

\section{The error bars of the fitted FCs}\label{sec:errors}
\paragraph{Derivation (least squares).} In \eqref{eq:filter}, $\by=\bV\mathbf F\bU^T\bb'$ with $\mathbf F=\mathrm{diag}\big(\sigma_k^2/\sqrt{\sigma_k^6+\sigma_c^6}\big)$.
If $\bb=\bb_{\rm true}+\boldsymbol\epsilon$ with independent errors of variance $s^2$ on every row, $\mathrm{Cov}(\boldsymbol\epsilon)=s^2\mathbf 1$ and
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Cov}(\by)=\bV\mathbf F\bU^T\,s^2\mathbf 1\,\bU\mathbf F\bV^T=s^2\,\bV\,\mathrm{diag}\Big(\frac{\sigma_k^4}{\sigma_k^6+\sigma_c^6}\Big)\bV^T,\qquad
\mathrm{Cov}(\bx)=\bK\,\mathrm{Cov}(\by)\,\bK^T
\end{equation}
($\bx_0$ is fixed, so it adds no variance). For $\sigma_k\gg\sigma_c$ this is the usual $s^2(\bK^T\bA^T\bA\bK)^{-1}$. The noise is
estimated from the fit itself, $s^2=\|\bA\bx-\bb\|^2/(m-n)$, $m$ rows and $n$ parameters. The standard error of FC $j$ is
$\sigma_j=\sqrt{\mathrm{Cov}(\bx)_{jj}}$ (\code{svd\_set}, \code{svbksb}, \code{fit\_kernel\_basis}).

\paragraph{By \code{enforce\_inv}.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{0} (invariance rows stacked on the force rows, one \code{svd\_set} of $[\bC;\bA]$, \code{focex.f90}): the formula
above with $\bK=\mathbf 1$; $m=n_C+N$ counts the invariance rows as rows with the same noise as the forces.
\item \textbf{1} (\code{fit\_kernel\_basis}): the formula above on $\bA\bK$; $m=N$, $n=n_K$.
\item \textbf{2 and 3} (\code{constrained\_fit.f90:lasso\_support\_errors}, called after the LASSO): least squares on the selected FCs
with the invariances exact, i.e.\ the formula above with $\bK$ replaced by $\bK\bN$, $\bN$ the null space of the rows of $\bK$
off the support (same $S$ test, \code{null\_space\_qr}), and $s^2$ from the residual of that least-squares fit ($n=p$).
For 3 this is the standard error of the returned refit. For 2 it is the error the refit would have; the returned FCs are the
LASSO values (the refit is computed but not applied; the log gives $\max|$refit $-$ LASSO$|$). FCs outside $S$ have
$\sigma_j=0$ (their rows of $\bK\bN$ vanish).
\item \code{itemp}\,=\,1 (Boltzmann-weighted fit): none, $\sigma_j=0$.
\end{itemize}
The log lists every irreducible FC with its $\sigma_j$, and $\mathrm{sd}(\rho)$ = the plain mean of $\sigma_j$ over the irreducible FCs
of rank $\rho$ (\code{ios.f90:write\_independent\_fcs}); for 2 and 3 the zeros of the dropped FCs are included in that mean.

\paragraph{What these error bars do not contain.} They are the covariance of a linear least-squares fit with independent equal
noise on all rows. They do not include the uncertainty of the selection $S$, the LASSO shrinkage (for 2), the L2 prior (the
error fit has no penalty), the choice of the cut, or model error (missing FCs, anharmonic terms beyond the fitted ranks):
when the misfit is model error, $s^2$ measures it but its rows are not independent. No Bayesian posterior is computed; the
standard errors coincide with the posterior standard deviations of a Gaussian likelihood with a flat prior, for directions
well above the cut.

\section{Why these choices}\label{sec:why}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Penalty on $\by$} (the previous \code{enforce\_inv}\,=\,2, still available as \code{FOCEX\_LASSO=old}): the columns of $\bK$ are dense
mixtures of FCs, so sparse FCs are dense in $\by$; on the exact model 98/80/376 non-zero for 11/20/43 true, FC4 wrong.
\item \emph{Elimination basis} (row-reduce $\bC$; the free FCs are a subset of $\bx$, as in ALAMODE): the result depends on which FCs
are eliminated (those carry no penalty); FC4 error 28 of 288. ALAMODE with LASSO keeps only the constraints within each
rank (\code{alm/constraint.cpp}); that left FC4 errors up to $1.2\times10^4$.
\item \emph{Free LASSO, then a constrained refit}: if the first step misses one true FC, the constraint rows containing it force its
partners to zero in the refit (reduced kernel of dimension 3, FC3 = FC4 = 0). Hence the selection itself is done with the
constraints (step 3).
\item \emph{Solver}: ADMM had not converged after 200\,000 iterations at 3 of 5 values of $\lambda$; FISTA needs the proximal map of
$\lambda\|\bQ\bt\|_1$, which has no closed form; LARS does not handle the constraint. The interior point needs 10--16
iterations and agrees with cvxpy/Clarabel to $10^{-11}$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Open points}
\begin{itemize}
\item The refit's Gram-matrix cut (\S\ref{sec:refit}) is coarser than \code{svdcut}; a QR-based refit would remove the difference.
\item The selection threshold $10^{-8}$ (step 3) is a numerical choice, tested only on the cases above.
\item The error bars of \S\ref{sec:errors} leave out the selection uncertainty; a bootstrap over snapshots would include it.
\item The tests should be rerun with the filter-penalty version.
\end{itemize}
